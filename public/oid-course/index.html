Operations, Information and Decisions for AI, Technology and Electrical Systems

A complete self-study course built from the Wharton School's PhD program in Operations, Information and Decisions, re-taught for an engineer working in AI, technology, and electrical systems. Every lesson covers the real models, theorems, and methods of its Wharton course — estimation theory, causal inference, game theory, mechanism design, stochastic control, optimization — with examples drawn from communications, control, networks, ML systems, and tech platforms, plus bridge lessons that connect the OID core directly to AI and EE.

How to take this course

  • Follow the parts in order. Part 0 is the mathematical warm-up; Parts 1–2 build the statistical and causal toolkit; Parts 3–4 cover strategic behavior and empirical methods; Part 5 covers human decision making; Part 6 covers information systems and ML; Part 7 covers operations, stochastic models, and optimization.
  • One lesson at a time. Each lesson states its time (45–75 minutes) and follows the same shape: motivation, learning objectives, taught core content, worked examples, key results, a self-quiz with answers, and further resources.
  • Do the examples. The worked examples carry the intuition — rework them with your own numbers before moving on.
  • Use the quizzes honestly. Try each check-your-understanding question before opening the answer.
  • Pacing. At one part per week, the course takes about two months; at a steadier research pace, treat each part as a two-week module.
