Available is not licensed: the second reader that froze part of my training corpus

P.Eng. — 24+ years, safety-critical energy systems

A second reader froze three sources out of my training corpus in one day. The rebuild that followed only ever removed — and nothing goes back in without a document.

What I am building, and what it eats

I am building a small electrical-engineering knowledge bank — worked problems, concepts, and limits drawn from the technical literature of my trade — and training models on it, slice by slice. Slice-003 closed on October 18: 302 examples, its test battery rebuilt clean after we found the pilot's own evaluation questions had leaked into the training slices. Slice-004 was next: 615 rows, staged and ready.
Every source that feeds that corpus gets a licence proof before its rows are written. I read those proofs myself. Until this week, mine was the only reading they got.

The second reader disagreed

This week the sources went through a second, independent reader — a different model, asked the same question I had asked: does this document actually grant the right to train on it? On four sources the two readings agreed, and those are banked. On three, the second reader disagreed with me — and on examination, the second reader was right.
The Sandia report carries an "unlimited release" stamp. I had read that as permission. It is not: it is an export and security clearance. It says the document may leave the building. It says nothing about copyright, and Sandia is contractor-operated, so the rule that makes U.S. government works public domain does not settle it either.
The NREL items are the same family. A contractor-operated national laboratory posts a reference design openly, through OSTI, for anyone to download. Availability is not a training licence. Nothing in the posting grants that right.
The SKM guides — twenty-two of them — carry a bare reproduction notice. I had treated the notice as a door left open. A notice that says a thing may be reproduced, without an express grant covering machine-learning training, is not an open licence. It is silence, and silence is not consent.
A fourth disagreement confirmed an exclusion I had already made: a text whose author died in 1961 stays out under the life-plus-seventy standard, until the end of 2031. The second reader reached the same wall by its own route. That, too, is information — agreement on an exclusion is worth as much as agreement on an admission.

What a freeze actually costs

All three sources were frozen out of the corpus the same day, before any training ran on slice-004. The dataset was then rebuilt — twice.
The first rebuild was removal only, and it took out 38 rows: 615 became 577. By source field, the frozen tags appeared on 23 rows sourced to NREL, 5 to Sandia, and 9 to SKM, some of them dual-sourced. Every surviving row was proven byte-identical to the row in the original — a removal that quietly rewrites the survivors is not a removal, it is a new dataset wearing the old one's name. The rebuilt file's licence gate was re-asserted in code: zero frozen sources, zero rows from the excluded licence classes, and the duty-bearing rows — the ones that teach the model to refuse what it was not trained on — went from 120 to 111.
The second rebuild existed because of a subtler problem. Ten of the surviving refusal rows still asserted that the frozen material was in the model's training. After the removal, that sentence was false. A corpus that teaches a model to make a false claim about its own training is a defect, even when the claim is small and the refusal around it is correct. So those ten rows — and only those ten lines; the diff proves it — were reworded to stand on the true ground: the model was not trained on this material, because its licence does not permit the use, because permission was not granted.
The dataset of record is now slice-004c, 577 rows. Training waits on that set, restaged fresh, and on a fresh launch decision. Nothing has been trained on the frozen sources. Nothing will be, on the strength of a stamp, a download page, or my first reading.

How a source gets unfrozen

Not by argument. By document.
For Sandia and NREL, the unfreezing evidence would be the paperwork behind the posting: the disclaimer pages and the intellectual-property clauses of the contracts under which the work was produced. If those grant the right, the sources come back, with the document filed beside the proof. For SKM, it is simpler and entirely human: an explicit permission ask, carrying the AI-training question in plain words. That letter sends only with my approval, and until it is answered, the guides stay out.
That is the standard I should have applied the first time: a source is admitted on a document that grants the right, not on the absence of a sign that forbids it. Publicly funded, publicly posted, and publicly downloadable are three true statements about a document. None of them is a licence.

The part that outlives this corpus

I have spent twenty-four years in heavy industry, where the equivalent error is familiar: a drawing stamped "released" is released for a stated purpose, and using it for another purpose because nobody wrote "no" on it is how plants get built wrong. A release stamp answers the question it was asked. It does not answer yours.
Two habits came out of this week, and both are now mechanical, not aspirational. Licence proofs get two independent readers, and a disagreement freezes the source until a document resolves it — the cheaper reader never overrules the stricter one by default; the stricter reading holds while the evidence is gathered. And every rebuild after a freeze is removal-only and proven so, byte for byte, with a wording pass afterward to hunt the claims the removal made false.
The artifact below is the gate I now run: feed it a dataset and the frozen-source list, and it fails loudly on any row that touches a frozen tag. Run it with the rebuild-proof flag and it also proves that a rebuilt dataset is the original minus the removed rows — every survivor byte-identical, in order, nothing edited, nothing added.
Download licence_gate_check.py — the gate itself:
#!/usr/bin/env python3 """Licence gate for a training corpus. Companion artifact to "Available is not licensed: the second reader that froze part of my training corpus" (nrupalakolkar.com/blog, Industrial Intelligence, 2026-10-19). Two jobs, both mechanical: 1. GATE — scan a dataset (JSONL, one row per line, each row carrying a "source" field) and fail loudly if any row touches a frozen source tag or an excluded licence class. "Available to download" is not a licence; a source stays frozen until a document — not an impression — unfreezes it. 2. REBUILD PROOF — given the pre-rebuild and post-rebuild datasets, prove the rebuild was removal-only: every surviving row is byte-identical to a row in the original, in the same order, and the count delta equals the number of frozen rows removed. A rebuild that quietly edits survivors is not a removal; it is a new dataset. Usage: python3 licence_gate_check.py dataset.jsonl frozen.txt python3 licence_gate_check.py --rebuild-proof before.jsonl after.jsonl frozen.txt frozen.txt format — one entry per line: TAG <tab or space> reason the source is frozen Blank lines and lines starting with # are ignored. Exit code 0 = gate clean / rebuild proven removal-only. 1 = findings. """ import json import sys def load_frozen(path): frozen = {} with open(path, encoding="utf-8") as fh: for line in fh: line = line.strip() if not line or line.startswith("#"): continue parts = line.split(None, 1) frozen[parts[0]] = parts[1] if len(parts) > 1 else "frozen" return frozen def row_sources(row): src = row.get("source", "") if isinstance(src, list): return [str(s) for s in src] return [str(src)] if src else [] def gate(path, frozen): findings = [] total = 0 with open(path, encoding="utf-8") as fh: for lineno, line in enumerate(fh, start=1): if not line.strip(): continue total += 1 try: row = json.loads(line) except json.JSONDecodeError as exc: findings.append(f"line {lineno}: not valid JSON ({exc})") continue for tag, reason in frozen.items(): if any(tag in s for s in row_sources(row)): findings.append( f"line {lineno}: touches frozen source {tag!r} " f"({reason})" ) return total, findings def rebuild_proof(before_path, after_path, frozen): findings = [] with open(before_path, encoding="utf-8") as fh: before = [ln for ln in fh if ln.strip()] with open(after_path, encoding="utf-8") as fh: after = [ln for ln in fh if ln.strip()] # Every survivor must appear byte-identical, in order, in `before`. it = iter(range(len(before))) pos = 0 for i, line in enumerate(after, start=1): found = False while pos < len(before): if before[pos] == line: found = True pos += 1 break pos += 1 if not found: findings.append( f"after line {i}: no byte-identical match in order in " f"the original — the rebuild edited or added a row" ) removed = len(before) - len(after) _, frozen_findings = gate(after_path, frozen) if frozen_findings: findings.append( f"rebuilt dataset still has {len(frozen_findings)} frozen " f"touch(es) — see gate run" ) print(f"before: {len(before)} rows; after: {len(after)} rows; " f"removed: {removed}") return findings def main(argv): if len(argv) == 5 and argv[1] == "--rebuild-proof": frozen = load_frozen(argv[4]) findings = rebuild_proof(argv[2], argv[3], frozen) return report(findings) if len(argv) == 3: frozen = load_frozen(argv[2]) total, findings = gate(argv[1], frozen) print(f"{total} rows scanned against {len(frozen)} frozen tag(s)") return report(findings) sys.exit(__doc__) def report(findings): for f in findings: print(" -", f) print(f"{len(findings)} finding(s)" if findings else "gate clean") return 1 if findings else 0 if __name__ == "__main__": sys.exit(main(sys.argv))
And the frozen-source list it checks against, one source per line:
# frozen.txt — one frozen source per line, with the reason SKM bare reproduction notice is not a licence NREL contractor-lab availability is not a training licence SANDIA unlimited release is export clearance, not copyright python3 licence_gate_check.py slice.jsonl frozen.txt python3 licence_gate_check.py --rebuild-proof before.jsonl after.jsonl frozen.txt
An honest corpus is slower to build than a large one. I will take the honest one. A model trained on material it had no right to learn from has the defect baked in at the foundation, and no amount of tuning on top of it changes what it is standing on.
