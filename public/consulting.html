Boards are being asked to govern AI without AI-fluent directors. I close that gap — independent oversight from someone who has operated systems that can't fail and builds the AI systems boards are asked to judge.
The problem
Management brings AI proposals to the board. Vendors bring AI promises. The board is expected to oversee both — usually without a single director who has built, broken, or operated an AI system. That gap is where reckless adoption happens: the wrong use case, the wrong vendor, the wrong risk accepted quietly.
Oversight doesn't need another framework deck. It needs judgment — someone who can tell diligence from hype, price technology risk the way an operator prices safety risk, and say plainly what the model doesn't know about your business.
What I offer
Board AI briefing
A 60–90 minute session for your board: what AI can and can't do in your industry, where the real risks sit, and what good oversight looks like. Leaves the board able to ask management sharper questions the same day.
AI governance readiness assessment
Where your organization stands on AI oversight — policies, accountability, risk controls — and a concrete roadmap to close the gaps. Informed by NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 thinking, fitted to how you actually run.
Technology diligence
An independent read on AI vendor claims, build-vs-buy decisions, or the technology behind a deal. I evaluate what the system actually does — not what the pitch says it does — and put the risks in plain language a board can act on.
Ongoing advisory retainer
A standing AI-fluent voice for your board or executive team: review AI proposals before they reach the board, sanity-check vendor claims, and keep oversight current as the technology moves.
AI for industrial operations
For operators bringing AI into maintenance, reliability, and operations — applied without losing the engineering discipline that keeps plants safe. The tool calculates, you decide.
